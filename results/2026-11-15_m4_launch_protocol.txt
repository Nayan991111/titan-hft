H61: 30 independent launches of titan-stream, 200,000 messages, 3 epochs each, 13 cases.
H62: 5 independent launches of titan-pipeline, BX from=34200 limit=100000, 5 epochs,
     once rates=13271 and once speed=1, waits wfe, spin, isb.
Unit of observation is the LAUNCH. Each launch is reduced to the median of its epochs per
case before anything is compared, and pad=64 is paired against pad=128 inside each launch so
that whatever the loader and allocator gave that process cancels in the difference.

Why the protocol changed. H3 found no effect, H3b found the reverse of its prediction and
H14 came back inconclusive at p = 0.69. The cause was written in results/2026-11-11_m4_spsc.txt
by that file itself: three epochs inside one process, on battery. Three epochs in one process
are not three samples of anything that depends on placement, and placement is the whole
question.

H61 (a) REFUTED. Uncached AcqRel streaming: pad=64 cheaper in cycles per message in 16 of 30
    launches, two-sided sign p = 0.8555, median paired difference +0.055 cycles with interval
    [-0.495, +0.625] straddling zero. There is no cycle effect either way.

(b) REFUTED. Cached AcqRel streaming: 14 of 30, p = 0.8555, median difference -0.090 cycles,
    interval [-0.480, +0.355]. No effect.

(c) REFUTED, and by a wide margin. The uncached AcqRel difference is 0.055 cycles against a
    pad=128 median of 24.630, which is 0.22% and not the registered 10%.

(d) SUPPORTED, and perfectly consistent. Uncached AcqRel L1D load misses per message are
    lower for pad=64 in 30 of 30 launches, sign p < 0.0001, median difference +0.311 misses
    with interval [+0.239, +0.387]. Sharing one 128-byte line does remove a miss per message,
    exactly as the coherence-granule argument predicts.

    But (d) does not explain (a) through (c), and saying so is the point. The two quantities
    are decoupled, in both directions. Where the L1D difference is cleanest, AcqRel, there is
    no cycle difference at all. Where the cycle difference is largest, SeqCst below, there is
    no L1D difference: +0.042 misses, 17 of 30, p = 0.5847. A measured miss on this core does
    not cost measurable cycles in this access pattern, and the cycles that are spent are not
    being spent on misses this counter can see. The mechanism registered in (d) is therefore
    confirmed as a fact and rejected as an explanation. This is the second mechanism in two
    days that measured true and explained nothing, the first being the depth profile in H60.

THE RESULT H61 WAS NOT LOOKING FOR, and the one worth the paper. The padding question has no
single answer: it depends on the memory ordering, and the dependence is large.

    uncached streaming, cycles per message, median over 30 launches
    ordering   pad=128    pad=64    paired difference        launches favouring pad=64
    acqrel      24.630    24.790    +0.055   (  0.2%)        16 of 30   p = 0.8555
    fenced      89.110    86.485    +4.335   (  4.9%)        23 of 30   p = 0.0052
    seqcst     167.280    93.920   +71.215   ( 42.6%)        29 of 30   p < 0.0001

    cached streaming, cycles per message, median over 30 launches
    acqrel      14.580    14.265    -0.090   ( -0.3%)        14 of 30   p = 0.8555
    fenced      26.730    27.420    -0.725   ( -2.7%)         8 of 30   p = 0.0161  (pad=128 wins)
    seqcst      15.595    14.025    +1.540   (  9.9%)        20 of 30   p = 0.0987

Under SeqCst without index caching, putting both indices in one 128-byte line is 42.6%
cheaper, in 29 of 30 independent launches. Under AcqRel the same change does nothing
measurable. Under Fenced the sign flips with caching: pad=64 wins uncached and pad=128 wins
cached, both significant. So the textbook instruction to pad to a coherence granule to avoid
false sharing is, on this core, correct only for some orderings and inverted for others.

Candidate explanation, offered as a hypothesis and not as a finding. Under SeqCst both sides
issue ldar loads and stlr stores on every message, and each side reads the other's index
constantly. With both cells in one line, a single coherence transfer carries both values; with
128-byte padding each side must obtain two lines. If the cost lives in coherence traffic and
ordering stalls rather than in cache fills, it would appear in cycles and not in L1D load
misses, which is the pattern measured. Testing this needs a coherence or snoop event rather
than L1D_LOAD_MISS, and whether the M4 PMU exposes one is unknown here. It is not claimed.

What this settles for H3b and H14. Their observation that pad=64 is faster in uncached
streaming reproduces strongly, but only for SeqCst (29 of 30) and Fenced (23 of 30), and not
at all for AcqRel (16 of 30). Neither of those statements named an ordering, so the question
is now answered per ordering rather than in general. Their weakness was the protocol, not the
observation: battery power and three epochs in one process.

H62 (a) SUPPORTED, but weakly, and the weakness is quantified below. Median p99 over 5
    launches, real timestamps against a constant 13,271 msg/s: isb 2,083 against 1,889 ns
    (1.103x), spin 2,088 against 1,913 (1.091x), wfe 2,018 against 1,870 (1.079x). All inside
    the registered 1.5x.

(b) REFUTED. The real-timestamp p99 does not hold within 1.25x across launches for every
    wait: isb spans 1,843 to 2,514 ns, a spread of 1.364x. spin (1.135x) and wfe (1.103x)
    pass; isb does not, and the part was registered for all three.

(c) REFUTED. The constant-rate arm fails too, and in a different wait: spin spans 1,877 to
    2,521 ns, 1.343x. isb (1.150x) and wfe (1.019x) pass.

    Both parts failing, in different waits, is more informative than either failing alone. It
    means the launch-to-launch instability is not a property of the burst structure, which
    was the alternative (c) was written to detect. It belongs to the machine and the harness
    at this latency scale, and it appears in whichever arm happens to draw a bad launch.

CONSEQUENCE, carried out as pre-registered rather than avoided. The arrival-structure effect
is 1.079x to 1.103x. The launch-to-launch noise floor on the same quantity reaches 1.364x.
The effect is smaller than the noise, so the direction cannot be established on this machine,
and H30b's verdict is weakened from supported to within the noise floor accordingly. H62 (a)
is reported as supported because its 1.5x threshold was met, while noting that a 1.5x
threshold cannot discriminate against a 1.364x noise floor and so carries little weight.

What is gained instead is the noise floor itself, measured rather than assumed: at a p99
around 2 us on this fanless machine, five independent launches of identical work differ by up
to 1.36x. Any future claim in this work about an effect smaller than that is not admissible
without more launches, and that bound now exists in writing.

ADDED 2026-11-16: what this run also says about memory ordering (RQ1), from the
same 30 launches and no new measurement.

This section is analysis of data already taken, not a pre-registered test. H61
registered its parts about padding, so the ordering comparison below carries no
registered threshold and is reported as an observation. It is recorded here
because it answers a question the log had left in a contradictory state.

Cycles per message above nothing, median over 30 independent launches, each
launch reduced to the median of its 3 epochs:

    index caching OFF
      pad=128   acqrel  24.630   seqcst 167.280 (6.79x)   fenced  89.110 (3.62x)
      pad=64    acqrel  24.790   seqcst  93.920 (3.79x)   fenced  86.485 (3.49x)
    index caching ON
      pad=128   acqrel  14.580   seqcst  15.595 (1.07x)   fenced  26.730 (1.83x)
      pad=64    acqrel  14.265   seqcst  14.025 (0.98x)   fenced  27.420 (1.92x)

The cost of the ordering is dominated by whether the consumer caches the
producer's index, not by the ordering itself. With the index cached, so that
each side reads the other's index once and then works locally until it runs
out, SeqCst costs between 0.98 and 1.07 times AcqRel: the two are the same
within the launch spread. Without that caching, SeqCst costs 3.79 to 6.79
times AcqRel. Fenced is between 1.83 and 3.62 times AcqRel and is the only
variant whose penalty survives index caching largely intact.

This resolves a contradiction. H1b states that in streaming, SeqCst being more
than 5% slower than AcqRel is refuted. That verdict is correct for the cached
configuration (1.07x and 0.98x) and wrong for the uncached one (6.79x and
3.79x), and the verdict names no configuration. It is the same defect H3b and
H14 had, which was naming no ordering, and it has the same remedy: the
question is answerable per configuration and not in general.

H1b is not re-verdicted here, because it has no results file at all and its
run used the preliminary protocol. What is recorded is that the question it
asked now has an answer from 30 launches on wall power, and that the answer
depends on a variable its statement did not mention.
