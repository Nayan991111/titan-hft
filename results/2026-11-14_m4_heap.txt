The array book answers both questions with one operation, and the two
stages agreeing to within 0.7 percent is the measurement of that: a price
level is already the sum of its orders, so depth costs nothing extra. The
heap pays 1.65x for the best price alone and more again for depth. Where
that goes is visible in the counters: L1D load misses per message are 6.45
for the array book, 17.3 for the indexed heap at the top and 158 for the
lazy heap at depth, because a heap of orders touches one cache line per
order while an array of levels touches one per level.

## a flaw in the first depth measurement, found on review

The depth figures above, and therefore parts (b) and (c), were measured
against a depth query that scanned the whole heap and kept the entries whose
key matched the best. That is not the best a heap can do. The heap property
makes keys non-increasing from the root down, so every entry holding the
best key is reachable from the root through entries that also hold it, and a
traversal that stops descending at the first smaller key visits those
entries and at most their immediate children. That is the number of orders
at the best price rather than the size of the heap.

This is the same error as the FIX decoder earlier today: our own structure
given its best form and the brief's given a weaker one. It is now fixed
(`HeapBook::best_depth` prunes, with a test that it finds every order at the
best price when all orders share it, when only the root holds it, and on the
ask side where the key is inverted), and H56 re-measures.

Parts (a) and (d) are untouched by this, because the `top` workload never
scans: the best price is the root. They stand as measured. Parts (b) and (c)
stand as measured against an unpruned scan, and the figure to quote for the
cost of depth in a heap is the one H56 produces, not the one here.

The size of the correction is predictable and is written into H56 as a test
of whether we understand our own numbers: BX held 42,994 live orders across
8,850 instruments at the end of the window, about five an instrument, so
there was almost nothing to prune and its ratio should barely move; the
synthetic feed held 982,360 across 501, about two thousand an instrument, so
its 11.563 should collapse.
