================================================================================
INTEGRITY OF THE RUN, CHECKED BEFORE ANY VERDICT WAS READ
================================================================================

900 data lines, 30 of 30 registered launches, 3 epochs, 10 cases. AC power held
at both ends. Low Power Mode off. No Chrome, no Docker, no powermetrics, no
Instruments.

The 180 sum_ok=0 lines are ALL the unordered arm, 90 uncached and 90 cached,
which is every one of its 30 launches times 3 epochs. The four ordered arms
reported sum_ok=1 in 180 of 180 lines each. No correct ring lost or duplicated
a message.

THAT IS A RESULT AND NOT ONLY A CHECK. The unordered arm is the same source
code on both machines. On this M4 it lost or corrupted data in 180 of 180
epochs. On the virtualized x86-64 host (E2) it reported sum_ok=1 in all 30
launches. Weak ordering breaks the ring observably on AArch64 and total store
order masks the same bug on x86-64. The premise that the ordering in this loop
is load-bearing is therefore measured, not assumed.

================================================================================
VERDICTS
================================================================================

H69 (a) THE SAVING IS STORE-DOMINANT.                               SUPPORTED
    Uncached, store-side share S = 1.0702 [1.0621, 1.0866] on cycles and
    1.0717 [1.0643, 1.0846] on nanoseconds, at or above the registered 0.70 in
    30 of 30 launches. The paired sign test on A - FS > 0 is 30 of 30,
    p < 0.00001, against the registered 21 of 30.

H69 (b) THE SAVING IS LOAD-DOMINANT.                                  REFUTED
    Uncached, load-side share L = 0.2797 [0.2729, 0.3479] on cycles, at or
    above 0.70 in 0 of 30 launches. The thresholds were set so that (a) and
    (b) could not both hold; they did not, and the design was forced to pick a
    side rather than report a direction.

H69 (c) THE TWO SIDES ARE ADDITIVE.                     REFUTED, AND SPLIT
    Uncached, S + L = 1.3587 [1.3461, 1.4063] on cycles and 1.3588 on
    nanoseconds, within the registered 0.15 of 1 in 1 of 30 launches. Strongly
    sub-additive: the two substitutions save 168.691 cycles separately and
    124.544 together.
    Cached, THE TWO REPORTING UNITS DISAGREE. On cycles S + L = 1.1133
    [1.1048, 1.1216], inside 0.15 of 1 in 28 of 30 launches, which is
    SUPPORTED. On nanoseconds S + L = 1.2333 [1.2059, 1.2470], inside in 1 of
    30, which is REFUTED. The cause is in the table above: the cached arms ran
    at 3.509 to 3.936 GHz, a 1.122x spread, so a ratio of differences in time
    is not a ratio of differences in work. Cycles are the primary unit for
    this project and the cached verdict is therefore SUPPORTED on cycles, with
    the disagreement recorded rather than resolved by preference.

H69 (d) EACH SIDE ALONE IS A MEASURABLE EFFECT.                     SUPPORTED
    Uncached, A - FL and A - FS both reach 30 of 30 in the same direction,
    both p < 0.00001, against the registered 21 of 30. The load side alone
    saves 35.080 cycles and the store side alone saves 133.611.

H69 (e) THE SIGN CHANGE REPRODUCES AND LOCALISES TO THE SAME SIDE.  SUPPORTED
    Cached F / A = 20.567 / 8.053 = 2.554, above the registered 2.0 and
    consistent with H68's 2.449. S is 1.1207 cached and 1.0702 uncached, both
    at or above 0.70, so the same side carries the effect on both sides of the
    sign change. One mechanism, not two.

H69 (f) THE UNORDERED ARM IS A USABLE FLOOR AT ALL.                 SUPPORTED
    Uncached (A - U) / A = +0.9534 [0.9532, 0.9539], positive in 30 of 30.
    Positive was the whole hypothesis after the x86 control measured the same
    quantity at -0.3596 and showed the original 0.30 size threshold to be
    malformed. On this machine the arm is a floor, and the figure it yields is
    large: ORDERING IS 95.3 PERCENT OF THE UNCACHED LOOP, 218.880 cycles
    against 10.185. Cached it is 17.5 percent.

H69 (g) THE RUN IS LONG ENOUGH TO BE READ.                 FAILED, AND THE
                                                           CHECK IS DEFECTIVE
    99 of 150 uncached arm-launches exceeded 200 ms, not 150 of 150.
    THE CHECK IS AT FAULT, FOR THE FIFTH TIME, AND THE FAULT IS THE ONE THAT
    WAS NAMED IN WRITING WHEN IT WAS REVISED. The revision of (g) stated the
    recurring error as "writing one global threshold for a quantity that
    differs per arm by design", and then set one global 200 ms threshold
    across five uncached arms that differ in cost by 21x by design. It failed
    on fencedstore at a 194.6 ms median, an arm whose clock was 4.368 GHz,
    squarely with the other three. So the check failed an arm that was
    perfectly ramped, while the arm it should have flagged, unordered at 27.6
    ms and 3.694 GHz, would also have failed for the right reason by accident.
    WHAT THE CHECK SHOULD HAVE BEEN, stated now and not applied to this run
    retroactively: the hazard is arms being compared at different clocks, so
    the threshold belongs on the clock spread across the arms in one
    comparison, not on the duration of any single arm. The measured spread is
    1.094x for the four ordered uncached arms and 1.122x within the cached
    set. The next registration that needs this check will state a clock-spread
    bound, pre-register it, and not reuse a duration threshold.
    THE VERDICTS ABOVE DO NOT DEPEND ON (g) PASSING. (g) is a validity gate on
    the clock, the clock table above answers the question directly and
    favourably for every comparison actually made, and the gate's failure is a
    defect in its own formulation rather than evidence against the run. This
    is stated as a limitation and not as a dismissal: a gate that has to be
    argued around after the fact is worth less than one that passes, and five
    failed attempts at this single check is the clearest measurement in this
    project of where its weakness lies.

================================================================================
WHAT THE DECOMPOSITION FOUND
================================================================================

Median cycles per message, 30 launches, pad=128, performance cores:

    UNCACHED                        CACHED
    unordered       10.185          unordered        6.646
    fencedstore     85.269          fencedload       8.021
    fenced          94.336          acqrel           8.053
    fencedload     183.800          fenced          20.567
    acqrel         218.880          fencedstore     22.120

THE ORDER OF THE FOUR ORDERED ARMS REVERSES COMPLETELY BETWEEN THE TWO
CONFIGURATIONS. The cheapest ordered ring when the cursor line is contended is
the dearest when it is in L1, and the other way round. fencedstore is 2.57x
cheaper than acqrel uncached and 2.75x dearer cached.

THE EFFECT IS ENTIRELY ON THE STORE SIDE. Replacing the release store `stlr`
with `dmb ish` and a plain `str` accounts for 133.611 of the 124.544 cycles
saved. Replacing the acquire load `ldapr` with `ldr` and `dmb ish` saves
35.080, which is real at 30 of 30 but is a quarter of the size and is the
secondary effect, not the mechanism.

POST HOC, NOT REGISTERED, AND LABELLED SO BECAUSE THE NUMBERS ASKED FOR IT.
The registration compares every arm against acqrel and never fenced against
fencedstore. That comparison is the sharpest thing in the run:
    uncached  fencedstore is 8.829 cycles CHEAPER than fenced, 29 of 30,
              p < 0.00001. Adding the load-side barrier on top of the
              store-side barrier COSTS 9.067 cycles, 10.6 percent.
    cached    fencedstore is 1.531 cycles DEARER than fenced, 0 of 30,
              so there the extra load-side barrier HELPS by 7.0 percent.
Being post hoc, this is reported as an observation requiring its own
registration before it is quoted as a tested result.

MECHANISM: A HYPOTHESIS, NOT A FINDING. A release store must not become visible
before prior accesses are observable. When the cursor line is being taken by
the other core, that obligation is discharged with a coherence miss in flight,
and the measured cost is consistent with the store stalling until it completes.
A `dmb ish` followed by a plain store pays a fixed barrier and then lets the
store retire into the store buffer with no further per-store obligation. That
would explain both the 2.57x saving when the line is contended and the 2.75x
penalty when it is not, since with the line in L1 there is nothing for the
release store to wait on and the barrier is pure overhead. IT IS NOT
ESTABLISHED. Confirming it needs either store-buffer occupancy counters or an
arm that varies the contention continuously, and neither is in this run.

WHAT THIS RUN CANNOT SETTLE. It cannot exclude a queue-occupancy explanation: a
barrier changes how far the producer runs ahead, so part of the difference
between these arms may be a difference in how full the ring sits rather than in
the cost of an instruction. Instrumenting occupancy inside the hot loop would
perturb what is being measured, so the alternative stays open. It is one Apple
part, one ring, one padding, one wait strategy, and performance cores only.
