Why this run exists. H57 and H58 measured what they claimed, but three things about how
they measured it would not have survived review, and all three were found by re-reading
the method rather than by anything in the data: the window was the opening 34.8% of the
session, a replacement was counted as a new order that ended unexecuted, and the phrase
"orders that ended" never stated its population.

A property of the whole session worth stating first: still_resting = 0. Every order that
arrived inside the session also ended inside it, and arrivals equal endings in all five
queue-position buckets. The population is therefore complete, with no truncation at
either edge, which is not true of any shorter window.

H59 (a) SUPPORTED. Of 10,629,593 order chains that both began and ended within the
    session, 10,110,283 never executed a single share: 95.11%, against a registered
    threshold of 50% and against the fourth brief's stated 50%. Only 4.89% saw any
    execution and 4.36% were filled in full. The earlier figure was therefore not an
    artifact of counting replacements.

(b) SUPPORTED. The chain rate is below the instance rate, as it must be, and the gap is
    0.79 percentage points: 0.9590 counting order references against 0.9511 counting
    chains, inside the registered 8 point ceiling. The accounting closes exactly:
    12,676,036 instances ended minus 10,629,593 chains equals 2,046,443, which is the
    replacement count to the message. Chains are short on average, 1.1925 instances, and
    only 3.21% were ever replaced at all, which is why the correction is small. The tail
    is not short: one chain was repriced 13,247 times inside the session.

(c) SUPPORTED. 40,367,075 of 46,884,746 attempts to fill 10,000 shares could not be
    filled from the displayed side at all, 86.10% against a registered threshold of 50%.
    At 1,000 shares the failure rate is 43.90%, worse than the 38.86% the opening third
    gave. At 100 shares the side almost always fills, failing 0.11% of the time.

(d) SUPPORTED, and this was the part that could have invalidated the seventeenth run.
    Every rate it covers moved by less than the registered 5 points between the opening
    10,000,000 messages and the whole session: chain never-executed 0.9700 to 0.9511, a
    move of 1.89 points; instance never-executed 0.9769 to 0.9590, 1.79 points;
    unfillable at 10,000 shares 0.8701 to 0.8610, 0.91 points. The opening window was
    representative, so the seventeenth run's rates were not biased by it. They are
    nonetheless superseded by the figures here, because these carry the whole session and
    a complete population.

The sentence this run exists to make safe to publish, with every term defined:
    Of 10,629,593 order chains that both began and ended within a full NASDAQ BX session
    on 30 July 2019, 95.11% never executed a single share, where a chain is an order
    together with every repricing of it.

The reversal H57 found at the first step survives the whole session and is larger here:
an order arriving with 1 to 99 shares ahead executes at 3.28 times the rate of an order
arriving with nothing ahead. From the second bucket on the series is monotone, and the
extremes separate by 7.00 times. The synthetic feed shows no reversal at all (0.1950,
0.1878, 0.1858, 0.1493, 0.0802), so the reversal is a property of real order flow: 82.6%
of real arrivals open a price level rather than join one, and being alone at a price the
market is not trading is worse than being second at a price it is.

Both per-share series remain non-monotone and remain confounded by survivorship for the
reason H58 recorded: the books that can fill the larger sizes are the deepest in the
sample. The confound is larger here, not smaller, because the exclusion rates are higher.
The conditioned test stays open and unclaimed.
