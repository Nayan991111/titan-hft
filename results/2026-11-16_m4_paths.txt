The engine was validated before it was measured, by ten tests whose oracles share none of its
machinery: uniforms against a chi-square over 64 bins (57.98 on 63 degrees of freedom);
normal variates against four moments and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic computed with the
cumulative normal that `pricing`'s tests validated against Simpson integration (KS 0.001624
against a 0.002150 critical value); the stepper against the exact moments of the process,
within 1.66 standard errors; a simulated European against Black-Scholes, worst 1.12 standard
errors over five contracts; a simulated geometric Asian against the exact closed form for
discrete averaging, worst 1.92; the barrier monitor against in-out parity path by path
(residual below 5.2e-15) and against an unreachable barrier (reproducing the plain generator);
and every pair of arms that must agree, agreeing.

The L1D column is not rounded down: it is zero to four decimal places in every arm on
performance cores, including the arm that writes and rereads 2 KiB per path. This engine is
arithmetic-bound, and every statement below is about the core's arithmetic throughput rather
than about this machine's memory system.

THE NUMBER THIS RUN CONTRIBUTES, and it is not one of the six registered parts. One
    exponential costs 7.65 to 7.69 cycles on a performance core, measured twice from
    independent pairs of arms:

        asian_arith minus asian_geom      7.647 cycles   (34.41 against 26.77)
        barrier_price minus barrier_log   7.693 cycles   (34.87 against 27.18)

    The two agree to 0.60 percent. Both differences are 30 of 30 launches with sign
    p < 0.0001 and bootstrap intervals narrower than 0.08 cycles. Against H65, which measured
    the whole cumulative normal at 15.87 cycles, this decomposes that figure: 7.67 for the
    exponential and 8.20 for the degree-five polynomial, the density evaluation and the sign
    branch around it. Two runs registered separately, three days apart, on different harnesses,
    cross-validate.

(a) REFUTED. The registered claim was that geometric averaging, being additive in the
    logarithm and so needing no exponential per step, costs below 0.70 of arithmetic
    averaging in cycles per step on performance cores. It costs 0.7778, with the geometric
    form cheaper in 30 of 30 launches, sign p < 0.0001, median difference 7.657 cycles
    [7.597, 7.683]. On efficiency cores the ratio is 0.598.

    The mechanism I registered was right and my magnitude was wrong. One exponential per step
    is removed, exactly as predicted. But I estimated an exponential at 13 to 16 cycles by
    reading across from H65's 15.87 for the whole cumulative normal, and it is 7.67. Against
    a step costing 34.41, removing 7.67 leaves 0.778, not 0.70. The registration's reasoning
    was sound and its arithmetic rested on a figure this run then measured directly.

(b) REFUTED, for the same reason and to within 0.2 percent of the same number. A barrier
    monitored in log space costs 0.7794 of the same barrier monitored in price space on
    performance cores, 30 of 30 launches, sign p < 0.0001, against a registered 0.70. On
    efficiency cores, 0.594. The two refutations are one refutation: both parts removed one
    exponential per step and both were registered against the same overestimate of what an
    exponential costs.

(c) SUPPORTED, and more strongly than the threshold asked. The registered claim was that
    branchless extremes are NOT faster than branchy ones, ratio above 0.95, because the theory
    of records puts a new extreme at about 1/k on step k, so the expected number of records in
    250 steps is about ln 250 = 5.5, or 2.2 percent of steps, and a branch taken two percent
    of the time is well predicted. The ratio is 0.9980.

    On performance cores the effect is not merely small but statistically absent: the
    branchless form is cheaper in 11 of 30 launches, sign p = 0.2005, and the bootstrap
    interval on the difference is [-0.109, +0.005] cycles, which contains zero. The
    instruction counts show where the 2.01 instructions per step the branchy form issues more
    of go: nowhere, because they are predicted. On efficiency cores a small real effect appears,
    0.985 with the branchless form cheaper in 30 of 30, worth 0.627 cycles of 41.67.

    This part is recorded as the registration's strongest result because it predicted that an
    optimization would fail, named the mechanism in advance, and the mechanism held.

(d) SUPPORTED. Stopping at the first touch costs 0.6657 of running to maturity in cycles per
    path on performance cores, 30 of 30 launches, sign p < 0.0001, against a registered band
    of 0.55 to 0.95. The validation suite had measured that early termination removes 46.2
    percent of observations at this barrier, so 0.538 is the floor a free saving would reach;
    the measured 0.666 sits above that floor, which is the registered prediction that the added
    test per step costs something real. The observation count confirms the work removed: 187.8
    of 250 observations taken, a 24.9 percent reduction, against a 33.4 percent reduction in
    cycles per path.

    A side effect worth recording: the early-exit arm mispredicts LESS when the barrier is near
    than when it is far, 0.295 against 0.385 per step, a ratio of 0.766 in 30 of 30 launches.
    Paths that knock out early execute fewer loop-condition branches, and that saving exceeds
    the cost of the barrier branch becoming unpredictable.

(e) REFUTED, in the opposite direction, and this is the run's most useful finding. The
    registered claim was that a stored path costs ABOVE 1.10 of rolling scalars, because 250
    doubles is 2 KiB of store and reload traffic and a second pass over it. It costs 0.9227:
    the stored path is 7.7 percent CHEAPER, in 30 of 30 launches, sign p < 0.0001.

    The second half of the part was registered precisely so that the first half could not be
    attributed to cache misses after the fact, and it is supported: L1D misses per step are
    0.0000 in both arms, below 0.05 in 30 of 30 launches. The 2 KiB fits, so nothing is paid
    for the traffic.

    The counters say why the fused loop loses. The two arms issue almost the same instructions,
    158.32 against 156.31 per step, a difference of 1.3 percent, but their IPC differs by 7.0
    percent: 4.429 fused against 4.740 split. In the fused loop the exponential sits on the
    dependency chain of the path recurrence, because the same `x` that the next step needs is
    the argument the exponential consumes. Splitting the loop breaks that: the first pass does
    only the recurrence, and the second pass evaluates exponentials whose arguments are all
    already known and therefore independent of one another, so they pipeline.

    The practical statement is the reverse of the advice that prompted this measurement.
    Storing the path and making a second pass is cheaper than fusing everything into one loop
    on this core, by 7.7 percent on performance cores and 27.5 percent on efficiency cores
    (53.12 against 73.25 cycles), and it costs nothing in cache misses at this path length.
    The advice to avoid storing paths for memory efficiency is, measured here, advice to pay
    7.7 percent for memory that was free.

(f) REFUTED by 3.0 percent, with the mechanism fully accounted for. The registered claim was
    that tracking every statistic costs below 1.50 of generating the path alone. It costs
    1.5447, in 30 of 30 launches, median difference 12.627 cycles [12.560, 12.665].

    The decomposition closes to the cycle. Rolling adds 12.604 cycles per step over bare
    generation. One exponential, measured independently above, is 7.647 of that, 60.7 percent.
    The remaining 4.957 is the log sum, the running minimum, the running maximum and the
    barrier comparison, four operations at about 1.24 cycles each. The registration said the
    exponential would dominate that list, and it does; it is simply not large enough relative
    to the other four to keep the total under half again the cost of the path.

    So every statistic a path-dependent payoff in this module needs costs 54 percent more than
    the path itself, not under 50. The claim the paper can make is the measured one.

POWER CONDITIONS DID NOT MATTER HERE, AND THAT REFINES THE PROTOCOL RATHER THAN BREAKING IT.
This run repeats, on AC power, a run made four hours earlier on battery, which its own
registration forbade. The two agree to within 0.16 percent on every absolute cycle count and
0.13 percent on every registered ratio:

    part                            AC      battery   AC/battery
    (a) asian_geom/asian_arith      0.7778   0.7775    1.0004
    (b) barrier_log/barrier_price   0.7794   0.7787    1.0009
    (c) lookback_free/branchy       0.9980   0.9967    1.0013
    (d) exit/full per path          0.6657   0.6660    0.9995
    (e) stored/rolling              0.9227   0.9226    1.0001
    (f) rolling/terminal            1.5447   1.5454    0.9996

The performance-core clock was 4.045 to 4.062 GHz on AC and 4.044 to 4.049 on battery, so the
machine did not downclock. H61 showed that preliminary conditions can reverse an answer; this
run shows that for a compute-bound single-core kernel on this machine the power source is not
one of those conditions. Both statements are compatible: H61's reversals were in a
latency-bound cross-core harness where the clock differed between arms.

Two limits on that conclusion, stated so it is not over-read. These runs are short, a few
milliseconds of load per launch, so they do not reach the sustained load at which a fanless
chassis throttles; nothing here says battery and AC agree under sustained load. And the
agreement is between two runs made four hours apart on the same machine in the same state
apart from the power source, not an experiment that varied power while holding everything else
fixed. The protocol's AC requirement is kept, because the cost of keeping it is nothing and the
cost of dropping it is a confound that would be invisible.

ONE OBSERVATION LEFT OPEN. Branch mispredicts sit at 0.390 per step in almost every arm,
including bare path generation, and the arms differ from each other by at most 0.010. That
floor is a property of the generator's loop structure rather than of any path statistic, and
this run does not establish which branch produces it. It does not confound the comparisons,
since it is common to every arm, but it is a large number to leave unexplained: at roughly 10
cycles a mispredict it would account for about 4 of the 23.14 cycles bare generation costs.
