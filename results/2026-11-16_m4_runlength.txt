A DEFECT IN THIS RUN'S OWN DESIGN, stated first because it changes how the streaming numbers
must be read. I registered `epochs=2`. The median of two epochs is their mean, so it averages
the cold first epoch in rather than selecting past it; H61, which these numbers are compared
against, used three epochs, whose median is the warm middle one. At 200,000 messages epoch 0
costs 217.190 cycles per message and epoch 1 costs 21.930, so the median of two is 119.560 and
is a figure for neither. Every streaming number below is therefore reported from epoch 1
alone, which is the quantity H61's median of three selected, and the median-of-two figures are
shown only where the difference is the point. Two epochs is the worst possible choice and the
registration should not have specified it.

THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE HYPOTHESIS: RQ1'S PUBLISHED ANSWER IS A 200,000-MESSAGE
ARTIFACT. The measuring core's clock in the warm epoch is 1.085 GHz at 200,000 messages,
2.790 at a million, 4.332 at five million and 4.315 at ten. A 200,000-message run finishes
before the frequency governor responds, so it is measured on a core at its idle clock. The
streaming harness has always been run at 200,000 messages. The tick-to-trade harness has
always been run at twenty million, which is why its figures reproduce and the streaming ones
do not.

RQ1 asks what memory ordering costs in a single-producer single-consumer ring. Measured at the
warm epoch, 128-byte padding, performance cores:

    SeqCst / AcqRel, index cached      SeqCst / AcqRel, index not cached
      200,000      1.187                  200,000      8.153
    1,000,000      1.366                1,000,000      1.530
    5,000,000      1.015                5,000,000      0.987
   10,000,000      1.030               10,000,000      0.997

    Fenced / AcqRel, index not cached
      200,000      3.934
    1,000,000      0.745
    5,000,000      0.460
   10,000,000      0.447

H61 reported 1.07x cached, 6.79x uncached and 3.62x for Fenced, all at 200,000 messages.

CORRECTION, added after H68 measured the same thing with three epochs: the sentence that stood
here claimed the cached half of RQ1 survives, on the ground that the SeqCst to AcqRel ratio is
1.015 and 1.030 at five and ten million messages. That ratio is right and the claim built on it
was too broad. It compares only the two acquire forms. The third ordering in the same table,
Fenced with the index cached, costs 20.480 cycles against AcqRel's 8.170 at ten million
messages, a ratio of 2.507, and H68 measures 20.735 against 8.465, a ratio of 2.449. The data
was in this run's own sweep and I did not check it before writing the conclusion. What survives
is narrower and is stated that way: between the two acquire forms the ordering is invisible with
the index cached at every run length tested. The explicit double barrier is not free when the
index is cached, and H68 records what it costs. The uncached half does not survive. At five and ten million messages
SeqCst and AcqRel cost the same to within 1.3 percent, in cycles and in nanoseconds alike.
The 6.79x exists only at the run length where the core has not ramped.

And the ranking inverts. Absolute cycles per message, warm epoch, uncached, 128-byte padding:

        messages      AcqRel      SeqCst      Fenced
         200,000      21.930     178.795      86.280
       1,000,000     133.860     204.815      99.750
       5,000,000     216.195     213.335      99.415
      10,000,000     221.990     221.250      99.185

    AcqRel's uncached cost grows 10.1x with run length while Fenced's grows 1.15x and is flat
    past a million messages. So at 200,000 messages Fenced costs 3.93 times AcqRel, and at ten
    million it costs 0.447 times AcqRel: two and a quarter times CHEAPER. The same three
    orderings, the same ring, the same machine, and the ordering that looks worst at one run
    length looks best at another.

    In nanoseconds the inversion is the same and is not a clock artifact: AcqRel uncached goes
    20.068, 47.642, 49.941, 51.007 ns while Fenced uncached goes 45.075, 33.587, 23.659,
    22.726 ns. One rises 2.5x and the other falls 2.0x.

WHAT IS AND IS NOT ESTABLISHED ABOUT THE MECHANISM. Two components are separable and only one
is explained.

    The clock ramp is established and quantified. At 200,000 messages the core is at 1.085 GHz
    and at five million it is at 4.33 GHz, a factor of 3.99. Any workload that spends time
    waiting on memory costs proportionally more CYCLES at a higher clock for the same
    nanoseconds, so a cycles-per-message figure from a short run is not comparable with one
    from a long run. The uncached arms carry 2.66 to 3.04 L1D misses per message and the
    cached arms 0.91 to 1.30, so the uncached arms are the ones this distorts most.

    The nanosecond-level divergence is NOT explained. AcqRel uncached slows by 2.5x in
    nanoseconds and Fenced uncached speeds up by 2.0x as run length grows, and L1D misses move
    by only 14 percent across that range, which is not enough to account for either. A
    plausible story is that the explicit `dmb ish` barrier prevents a speculative behaviour
    that the `ldapr` path permits and that only becomes pathological once the stream is long
    enough, but nothing here measures that. It is recorded as the project's largest open
    question and is not claimed.

WHAT MUST CHANGE, LISTED SO IT IS NOT LOST.

    1. Every streaming figure in the README and in H1, H1b, H2, H3b, H14 and H61 was measured
       at 200,000 messages and is a figure for an unramped core. The cached conclusions hold.
       The uncached ordering comparison does not, and the Fenced ranking reverses.
    2. RQ1 needs re-measuring at a run length the feed implies. One NASDAQ BX session is
       28,734,686 messages, so five to ten million is the right order and two hundred thousand
       is not.
    3. Nothing in the tick-to-trade results is affected. Those were always run at twenty
       million messages and all three stages reproduce within 0.6 percent.
    4. No results file is withdrawn. H61's numbers are correct for the run length they were
       measured at, and that run length is now stated alongside them. What changes is the
       generality claimed for them.
