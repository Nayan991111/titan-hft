What waiting costs in energy on a fanless chip (H63)
Apple M4 MacBook Air (Mac16,13), macOS 26B123, rustc 1.92.0, commit 464c0a4 (tree 7eb2439; was 6692228 before the author-name rewrite of 2026-11-17), 2026-11-15 20:52 IST.
AC power, Low Power Mode off, Docker quit, no thermal or performance warning recorded at any
point across all three launches. No PMU in the binary at all: powermetrics seizes the Apple
Silicon counters, so the pipeline ran without kperf and power was measured from outside the
process. One powermetrics process per arm at 200 ms, stopped when the arm ended, so each raw
file holds only that arm's samples; first and last sample of each arm dropped. 21 arms over 3
launches: idle, then wfe, spin and isb at 13,271 msg/s and at 20,000,000 msg/s offered.
53 to 177 samples per arm, 10.9 to 36.3 s per window.

This is the first energy measurement anywhere in this work. No wait-strategy result before it
reported power, only latency and its tail.

H63 (a) SUPPORTED. At 13,271 msg/s, wfe draws less CPU power than both spin and isb in all 3
    launches, measured above that launch's own idle baseline:
        launch 1   wfe 2734.4   spin 6572.5   isb 4655.4   mW above idle
        launch 2   wfe 1859.0   spin 5275.3   isb 3667.6
        launch 3   wfe 2702.2   spin 6194.7   isb 4601.9
    The ordering also holds on the raw figures with no subtraction at all, which matters
    because the baseline is unreliable (see (e)).

(b) SUPPORTED. Median of the per-launch spin to wfe ratios is 2.404 against a registered
    threshold of 2 (2.403, 2.838, 2.292 by launch). Robust to how the statistic is formed:
    the ratio of medians is 2.292 and the ratio of raw unsubtracted means is 2.232. All three
    clear 2.

(c) SUPPORTED, and by a wide margin. At 20,000,000 msg/s offered, the three waits fall within
    1.0021x, 1.0225x and 1.0153x of each other by launch on the raw figures, and within
    1.0021x, 1.0307x and 1.0158x above idle, against a registered 1.25x. So the difference in
    (a) belongs to waiting and not to the work. Means across launches: wfe 4003.8, spin
    4015.5, isb 4048.1 mW.

(d) REFUTED. P-cluster active residency at 13,271 msg/s is not lower for wfe in all 3
    launches: launch 2 has wfe at 90.6% against spin 90.1% and isb 90.2%, so wfe is the
    highest of the three there. Launches 1 and 3 pass (89.0 against 90.4 and 90.5; 90.3
    against 90.4 and 90.6).

    The refutation understates what the numbers say, and the real result is this. Mean
    P-cluster active residency at 13,271 msg/s is 90.0% for wfe, 90.3% for spin and 90.4% for
    isb. They are the same to within half a percentage point, while the power differs by
    2.26x. Mean P-cluster active frequency is 3965.2 MHz for wfe, 3730.3 for spin and 3977.2
    for isb, so wfe draws less than half the power of spin while running at a HIGHER clock
    than spin.

    Parking the core on the exclusive monitor therefore saves energy without the core
    becoming idle in any sense macOS reports, and without clocking down. The saving is in
    what the core executes while waiting, not in whether it is awake: spin and isb retire
    load instructions continuously, wfe issues one and stalls on the monitor. Residency is
    the wrong proxy for energy on this core, and anyone profiling Apple Silicon wait
    strategies through residency alone would conclude the three are equivalent when one of
    them costs less than half as much.

    This is the third proposed mechanism in three days that measured true and explained
    nothing, or measured nothing while the effect was large: the depth profile in H60 (c),
    the L1D load misses in H61 (d), and residency here. All three are recorded as such. On
    this core the intuitive counter-visible proxy repeatedly fails to account for the
    measured effect, and that pattern is itself a methodological finding.

(e) REFUTED, by the baseline arm and by no other. Launch-to-launch spread per arm against the
    registered 1.25x:
        idle      40.4 to 1052.9 mW    26.062x   FAIL
        low wfe  2774.8 to 2911.9       1.049x   pass
        low spin 6328.2 to 6612.9       1.045x   pass
        low isb  4695.8 to 4735.4       1.008x   pass
        sat wfe  3947.4 to 4114.2       1.042x   pass
        sat spin 3940.1 to 4105.8       1.042x   pass
        sat isb  4000.3 to 4105.6       1.026x   pass
    Every arm that carries a comparison is stable to within 4.9%. The idle arm is not, and
    the cause is identifiable: the script warned that Google Chrome was running and it was
    not quit. Launch 2's idle window shows P-cluster residency of 8.9% and E-cluster 29.6%
    against launch 1's 0.0% and 5.3%, so that window caught background work rather than a
    machine at rest.

    Why the verdicts survive it. The measurement arms draw 2.8 to 6.6 W while the baseline is
    0.04 to 1.05 W, so the baseline is a correction of 1% to 16% rather than the signal, and
    every verdict above was checked both with and without subtraction and does not change.
    The figure that cannot be trusted from this run is absolute energy per message, which
    depends on the baseline; the figures that can are the ratios between waits at the same
    load, which do not.

Energy per message, reported as an upper bound and not as a measurement. Each arm's window
includes the binary's own setup, chiefly reading an 837 MB feed file, so some of the measured
energy is not attributable to message processing. Above each launch's own idle baseline:
        13,271 msg/s      wfe 165.9 to 248.4 uJ   spin 470.8 to 596.9   isb 327.3 to 422.8
        20,000,000 msg/s  wfe 0.317 to 0.446 uJ   spin 0.319 to 0.445   isb 0.323 to 0.446
Taking medians, a message costs about 581 times more energy under wfe at feed rate than at
saturation, and about 1,335 times more under spin. The overhead inside the window is
proportionally larger in the saturating arm, roughly half of a 10.95 s window for about 5 s of
work at the achieved rate, so these multiples are understated rather than overstated.

Two limitations recorded rather than left for a reviewer to find. First, the saturation flag
could not be extracted from the run output because the pattern used to pull it matched an
empty string, so saturation is not directly confirmed; the achieved rate was 19.42 to 20.00
million messages per second against 20.00 offered, which places the harness at or very near
its limit, and the near-identical power across the three waits is itself the evidence that
waiting is not differentiating them there. Second, P-cluster residency in the saturating arms
is 69.3% against about 90% in the low-rate arms, which is the opposite of what the load
implies and is attributable to the same setup time sitting inside the window; it is a reason
to treat the saturating arm's absolute figures with care and not its comparisons.

The usable result for the paper. At the real feed's rate on a fanless MacBook Air, the
trading core draws 2.84 W waiting on the exclusive monitor, 4.72 W spinning with a pipeline
hint, and 6.42 W spinning bare. The pipeline hint recovers 26.6% of the gap between bare
spinning and wfe, and wfe costs 2.26x less than bare spinning. At saturation the three are
indistinguishable within 3%. None of this is visible in P-cluster residency, which reads
about 90% in all three cases.
