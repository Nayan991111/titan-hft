The cost of immediacy over one fixed population, and the depth shape (H60)
Apple M4 MacBook Air, macOS 27.2, rustc 1.92.0, commit e052fc5, 2026-11-15 12:34 IST.
No PMU and no timing hypothesis in this batch, so the run needed no elevated privilege
and thermal state does not bear on any verdict here: every quantity is an exact count or
mean over a stated population. Real feed: NASDAQ BX TotalView-ITCH 5.0, 2019-07-30, all
28,734,686 messages, 8,850 instruments, 46,884,746 quotable sides. Synthetic feed
reported as contrast. Zero allocations in every pass.

This run closes the survivorship confound that H58 recorded and did not resolve.

H60 (a) REFUTED. Over the fixed population, the quote move per share does not rise across
    the five sizes. Per 100 shares: 0.0743, 1.0391, 1.3571, 0.6732, 0.9337 basis points.
    It rises twice, then reverses at 1,000 to 5,000 shares, 1.3571 down to 0.6732.

(b) REFUTED. The average price paid per share does not rise either. Per 100 shares:
    0.0488, 0.5252, 0.6100, 0.3922, 0.3673. Two rises, then reversals at 1,000 to 5,000
    and again at 5,000 to 10,000.

    What this refutes is stronger than a wrong exponent, and it is worth stating exactly.
    The brief writes the change in price as a constant times size raised to a power. Under
    that form the cost per share is the constant times size raised to the power less one,
    which is monotone in size for every value of the power: rising above one, falling
    below one, flat at one. A measured cost per share that rises and then falls cannot be
    produced by any exponent. So the functional form is refuted on this venue, not merely
    the value the brief assigns to its exponent. The peak sits near 1,000 shares.

(c) REFUTED, and this is the most useful number in the run. Mean shares per level over
    the fixed population, from the touch outward: 5103.6, 6297.5, 5809.0, 4799.1, 3554.6,
    2668.1, 1990.1, 1671.8. Depth peaks at the second level and then thins at every
    further level, falling 73.45% from the second to the eighth, a factor of 3.767. The
    registered statement was that it does not fall. It falls.

    The synthetic feed does the opposite. Its depth thickens monotonically at all eight
    levels, 502.5 to 1789.7, a factor of 3.562. The two feeds therefore have inverted
    depth shapes, and the consequence shows up directly in the systems measurement: a
    walk to fill 10,000 shares visits 4.344 levels on the real feed and 10.772 on the
    synthetic one. A synthetic generator of this kind does not reproduce the shape of a
    real book, so order book code benchmarked only on synthetic feeds is being measured
    against depth that thickens outward when real depth thins outward.

    A second correction, against my own registered reasoning rather than against the
    brief. Part (c) assumed that a mean depth profile fixes the cost-versus-size shape.
    It does not, and the data shows why: mean depth at the first level over this
    population is 5103.6 shares, yet filling 1,000 shares still takes 1.478 levels on
    average, and filling 10,000 takes 4.344. The mean is inflated by very deep sides,
    so what governs the cost at a given size is the distribution of depth at the touch
    across sides and not its average. The mechanism in (c) is therefore weaker than
    registered, and the non-monotone cost in (a) and (b) is not explained by the mean
    profile alone. That explanation is not attempted here and is not claimed.

(d) SUPPORTED. Conditioning changes the shape, so the confound H58 recorded was material
    and the caveat was warranted. Unconditioned, both per-share series rise once and then
    fall at every later size, peaking at 500 shares: move 4.5826, 39.3685, 16.6646,
    1.1944, 0.9337 and paid 2.8129, 14.1828, 3.9299, 0.3854, 0.3673 per 100 shares.
    Conditioned, both rise twice and peak at 1,000 shares. The peak moves by one size
    step and the pattern of rises and falls differs.

Consequence for H58, recorded rather than left implicit. H58's (a) and (b) keep their
refuted verdicts and keep the confounded label, because (d) establishes the confound was
real. They are superseded for quotation by H60 (a) and (b), which measure the same two
series over one population and refute the brief's form cleanly. The open item H58 created
is now closed.

The fixed population: 6,517,671 sides, 13.90% of all quotable sides. Fillability rises
with a side's total, so these are exactly the sides that can fill every one of the five
sizes, and all five series share this denominator. On the synthetic feed the same
condition holds for 97.64% of sides, which is why conditioning barely moves its numbers:
its per-share series stay monotone falling both before and after conditioning (paid
0.3771, 0.2197, 0.1792, 0.1002, 0.0717 per 100 shares), consistent with its thickening
depth and with a power below one. The real feed admits no such exponent.
