H57 (queue position, and the cancellation rate the fourth brief states)

(a) REFUTED. The share of orders that ever execute is not non-increasing across the five
    queue-position buckets on the real feed. The reversal is at the first step: orders
    arriving with nothing ahead of them execute at 0.0266, while orders arriving with 1
    to 99 shares ahead execute at 0.0961, which is 3.61 times higher. From the second
    bucket onward the series is monotone as registered: 0.0961, 0.0158, 0.0046, 0.0030.
    The reversal is a property of the real feed, not of the measurement: on the synthetic
    feed, where order flow is random by construction, the series is monotone with no
    reversal at 0.1950, 0.1878, 0.1858, 0.1493, 0.0802.

    Why the registered direction failed. Shares ahead conflates two different things:
    position in a queue, and whether the price has a queue at all. 3,172,917 of the
    4,429,141 arrivals on BX, which is 71.6%, arrived with nothing ahead, meaning they
    opened a price level rather than joined one. An order alone at a price is first in
    line at a price the market is not trading, and it fills less often than an order
    second in line at a price the market is trading. Conditional on the level already
    existing, the brief's mechanism holds exactly as stated.

(b) SUPPORTED. 0.0266 at the front against 0.0030 with 10,000 or more shares ahead, a
    ratio of 8.87, against a registered threshold of 2. Parts (a) and (b) are consistent:
    the extremes separate strongly, the ordering in between is not monotone.

(c) SUPPORTED. 4,284,968 of 4,386,147 orders that ended inside the window ended without
    any execution at all, a rate of 0.9769. The brief states 50%. The venue aggregate is
    nearly double it. Registered caveat, restated: ITCH names every order but no firm, so
    this is the rate across all participants and is consistent with the brief's claim
    about high frequency firms without establishing it. Only 0.0231 of ended orders saw
    any execution and 0.0210 were filled in full.

(d) SUPPORTED. X(queue) / X(book) = 1.092 [1.082, 1.101] on BX, against a registered
    ceiling of 1.35. Synthetic 1.205 [1.193, 1.218]. X(book) = 91.25 [91.04, 91.74]
    cycles per message above decoding, X(queue) = 99.67 [98.89, 100.48]. Instructions
    rose from 168.73 to 187.30 per message while cycles rose 9.2%, and IPC rose from
    1.486 to 1.545: the extra work is the binary search the add already performs, run a
    second time on a level that is still in cache.

H58 (what immediacy costs, and the brief's power above one)

(a) REFUTED. The quote move needed to fill a size, per share, does not rise across the
    five sizes on the real feed. Measured at 100, 500, 1000, 5000, 10000 shares:
    4.188, 31.538, 17.026, 1.666, 1.258 basis points per 100 shares. It rises once, from
    100 to 500, then falls at every later step.

(b) REFUTED. The average price paid per share does not rise either: 2.402, 11.314,
    3.844, 0.525, 0.591 basis points per 100 shares, with the same single rise and then
    a fall.

    Confound, recorded rather than argued away. These two series cannot cleanly test the
    brief's claim, and the reason is in the data beside them. A size that the displayed
    side cannot fill is excluded from the cost at that size, and the exclusion is not
    small: 0.08% of sides cannot fill 100 shares, 18.50% cannot fill 500, 38.86% cannot
    fill 1000, 78.89% cannot fill 5000 and 87.01% cannot fill 10000. The books that
    survive to the larger sizes are the deepest and tightest in the sample, so the mean
    at 10000 shares is taken over a different and far more liquid population than the
    mean at 100. The refutation above is of the registered statement on the measured
    numbers; it is not evidence that the brief's exponent is below one. A clean test
    would need the cost conditioned on a fixed set of instrument intervals, which this
    run does not do and which is recorded here as the open item.

    What survives the confound. At 100 shares, which 99.92% of sides can fill, the cost
    of immediacy on BX is 2.40 basis points on average and the quote moves 4.19 basis
    points, over 15,618,175 measured sides. The worst single case at that size is 4830
    basis points.

(c) SUPPORTED. 13,600,453 of 15,630,442 attempts to fill 10,000 shares could not be
    filled from the displayed side at all, a rate of 0.8701, against a registered
    threshold of 0.01. The brief's worked example walks a large order down a ladder of
    depth; on this venue that ladder is absent in seven cases out of eight. Even 1,000
    shares cannot be filled 38.86% of the time. The synthetic feed, by contrast, fails
    at 10,000 shares only 2.36% of the time, which is what an arbitrary generator
    produces and what a brief written without data assumes.

(d) SUPPORTED. X(walk_10000) / X(top) = 1.667 [1.663, 1.683] on BX, against a registered
    floor of 1.5. X(top) = 96.99 [96.38, 97.26], X(walk_100) = 105.79 [105.33, 106.20],
    X(walk_1000) = 147.78 [146.46, 148.75], X(walk_10000) = 161.72 [161.53, 162.91]
    cycles per message above decoding.

Finding beyond the verdicts: the walk costs far less on the real feed than on the
synthetic one, 1.667 against 2.140 times the best-price query, and the reason is the same
thinness that refutes the brief's ladder. A walk to 10,000 shares visits 4.512 levels on
average on BX and 10.772 on the synthetic feed. On a real venue the walk is cheap because
there is little to walk. The two facts are one fact seen from the systems side and from
the market side.
