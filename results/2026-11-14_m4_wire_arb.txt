## verdicts under the pre-registered rules

H50 (inbound wire format), supported on both feeds, all three parts.
  (a) X(fix) / X(bin) at least 5: BX 55.49 [55.22, 56.05], synthetic 44.35
      [44.17, 44.84]. Supported.
  (b) X(json_serde) / X(json_fixed) at least 2: BX 4.57 [4.53, 4.61],
      synthetic 4.33 [4.27, 4.36]. Supported.
  (c) json_fixed below fix in total cycles: BX 163.23 against 522.45,
      synthetic 167.42 against 518.52, both p = 0.0000 (exact Mann-Whitney,
      n = 10 per side), medians in the stated direction. Supported.
  Reported, not tested: allocations per update are 1.0000 for json_serde and
  0.0000 for the other three decoders, on both feeds and in every epoch.
  Bytes per update: binary 32.00; FIX 129.45 synthetic, 124.90 BX; JSON
  98.13 synthetic, 95.95 BX. Every decoder returned the original record for
  all 2,000,000 updates before timing (asserted), and the four decode stages
  of a feed printed one identical checksum (synthetic 4baa7350b00781fc, BX
  b06eb7f33ad1cb53).
  The thresholds of (a) and (b) were set after development-machine runs, as
  the pre-registration discloses; the measured ratios exceed them by a wide
  margin on hardware the thresholds were not drawn from.
  Qualification, added when the run was reviewed: the FIX decoder measured
  here finds delimiters and sums the checksum one byte at a time, while the
  binary decoder has no such loop. This repository already showed that a NEON
  delimiter search cuts a validated FIX 4.2 parse from 465 to 301 cycles
  (results/2026-11-12_m4_ac2.txt), so the ratios above are against an
  unoptimised text parser and overstate the gap a best-effort implementation
  would leave. H52 (twelfth run) measures that best effort; until it is run,
  the figure to quote for FIX is the one measured here with that
  qualification attached.

H51 (A/B arbitration and gap recovery): (a) partly supported, (b) supported,
(c) supported.
  (a) X(two0) / X(one0) at most 1.5: BX 1.429, synthetic 1.670. The rule
      holds on BX and fails on the synthetic feed, so partly supported.
      Disclosure: the bootstrap 95% interval on BX is [1.358, 1.586] and so
      reaches above the 1.5 bound; the registered rule is on the ratio of the
      medians, and that is how the verdict is read here. The synthetic feed
      has fewer, larger messages per packet, so each duplicate packet is
      amortised over fewer delivered messages.
  (b) X(two_ab) / X(two0) at most 2.0: BX 0.892 [0.799, 0.973], synthetic
      0.851 [0.800, 0.963]. Supported.
      Limitation, stated plainly: this test was weak as registered. Loss
      removes packets from the arrival stream, so the lossy conditions
      process fewer packets than the lossless two-line condition and come out
      cheaper than it (both ratios are below 1). The threshold of 2.0 could
      hardly have been crossed. The quantity that would carry information is
      the cost of a recovery event with the packet count held constant; this
      run does not measure it, and the claim made here is only the registered
      one: recovery does not multiply the per-message cost.
  (c) Exact delivery in every condition and epoch of both feeds: supported.
      Within a feed, all four conditions printed one identical delivery
      checksum in all 10 epochs (synthetic 07b91b3ddcc13928, BX
      b328fb892a73f302), and the untimed pass asserted message-for-message
      equality with the store. Recovery was exercised: 50 requests and 2,236
      resent messages on the synthetic feed, 47 and 2,351 on BX, with no
      partial response and no foreign packet. allocations_pass is 0 for every
      condition in every epoch of both feeds.
  Exploratory, not a registered claim: a lossy second line costs less per
  message than a perfect one (two_a and two_ab below two0 on both feeds,
  p = 0.0433 for three of the four contrasts), which follows from the same
  arrival-count mechanism as the limitation above.
