## what the round-lot view changes

The episodes do not merely become fewer; they become a different phenomenon.

                               every displayed order    round-lot levels
  crossed episodes                       33                     2
  instruments ever crossed               17                     2
  total crossed time                602.1 s                 0.000 s
  longest crossed episode           212.8 s                0.110 ms
  locked episodes                       360                    82
  total locked time               6,156.2 s                0.047 s
  longest locked episode          1,208.0 s               43.8 ms

So the twenty-minute lock and the three-minute cross of the previous run
were odd-lot orders resting inside the other venue's quote, exactly as the
fourteenth run's pre-registration proposed and the thirteenth run's results
file warned. Between round-lot-sized levels the two venues of 2019-07-30
crossed twice in a trading day, for 110 microseconds at most, and locked for
44 milliseconds at most. That is the behaviour Rule 610(d) describes, and
the residual durations are of the order of the time it takes one venue to
react to the other rather than of the order of a tradable opportunity.

Stated plainly, because the first version of this measurement said
otherwise: the apparent cross-venue arbitrage in a consolidated BX and PSX
book is almost entirely an artifact of counting odd lots, which are not
protected quotations. No claim is made here that either remaining episode
was tradable; the replay still uses each venue's own clock and models no
propagation delay between them.

Reported, not tested, and the reason the two halves of this experiment
belong in one paper: a consolidated view rebuilt every 1,000 messages missed
both of the round-lot crossed episodes, a share of 1.000, because at 110
microseconds they are far shorter than the 224 millisecond mean interval
between rebuilds inside the window. The incremental view, which costs 22.5
cycles per message against the 157.7 of the two venue books alone, sees
every one of them. The design that is 5.3 times cheaper per message is also
the only one that observes the event at all.

The staleness accounting is now restricted to the window, as the fourteenth
run said it would be: the longest a change waited for its rebuild falls from
49.5 minutes to 1.703 seconds, and the mean is 224.0 ms, which is half the
400 ms that 1,000 messages take at the 2,478 messages a second this day
averaged inside the window, as a uniform arrival rate would give.
